### Goethe A1 adult exam overview

- Hören: about 20 minutes — short conversations, phone messages, announcements.
- Lesen: 25 minutes — notes, advertisements, signs, short texts.
- Schreiben: 20 minutes — simple forms and short personal messages.
- Sprechen: 15 minutes — introduce yourself, exchange simple information and make requests.
- Official Start Deutsch 1 pass threshold: 60/100 overall, provided all sections are attempted. Do not apply this scale to every Goethe level; use level-specific rules.

Sources: https://www.goethe.de/ins/eg/de/sta/alx/prf/gzsd1/inf.html and https://www.goethe.de/de/spr/prf/pes.html

### Critical commercial SaaS rights

- Publicly downloadable Goethe PDFs, test audio and exam questions are **not automatically licensed** for inclusion in a paid SaaS.
- Hueber requires a usage agreement before uploading its material to a learning platform. See: https://www.hueber.de/en/ueber-uns/kopierregeln-digitaler-unterricht
- ZUM Deutsch Lernen states its material is generally CC BY 4.0, allowing commercial adaptation with attribution, but verify individual assets and any exceptions.
- For CEFR, use descriptors to plan your outcomes but avoid wholesale redistribution of the Council of Europe publication.
- Prefer **original German texts, dialogues, recordings, answer explanations and mock exams** with specialist German review.
- Never market unofficial tests as official Goethe exams, or display Goethe branding in a way implying affiliation.

## 3. Exam techniques you can teach inside the app

### Listening — Hören

1. Read the question before listening; identify what you need (name, number, time, place, intention).
2. Expect distractors and changed plans. The first number or date heard may not be the answer.
3. Learn to recognize paraphrases; the audio might not repeat the answer choice verbatim.
4. In strict mock mode, match the listening attempts and pauses of the official exam model; in practice mode, allow replay and transcript review.
5. After finishing, re-listen with transcript, save misunderstood words, practise dictation.

### Reading — Lesen

1. Read the question first, then scan the text for relevant details.
2. Watch for negatives such as `nicht`, `kein`, `geschlossen` and for time conditions.
3. Confirm meaning rather than matching one identical word.
4. Use a timer in exam mode; reveal explanations only afterward.

### Writing — Schreiben

1. Identify every requested content point and tick each off while drafting.
2. Keep basic messages simple and accurate rather than using grammar you cannot control.
3. Practise greetings, introductions, making requests, thanking and signing off.
4. Check verb position, article, noun capitalization and question marks.
5. Assess task completion separately from grammatical accuracy.

### Speaking — Sprechen

1. At A1, practise an introduction using name, age, country, city, languages, profession and hobbies.
2. Train question forms, then make requests and respond politely.
3. Use repair phrases: `Wie bitte?`, `Können Sie das bitte wiederholen?`, `Langsam, bitte.`
4. Record one-minute practices and listen for pauses, intelligibility and whether the response answers the task.
5. Practise with another human when possible; AI transcription alone cannot certify pronunciation or official scores.

## 4. Suggested app feature design

### Exam resource directory

Each entry:

- certificate level / provider
- skill, source title, source URL
- original model or supplementary practice
- audio/video/answers availability
- rights status: `link_only`, `permission_requested`, `licensed`, `original`
- checked_at and next_review_at

### Goethe Exam Arena

- Exam Explorer: section descriptions and official resource links
- Skill Drills: original exercises reviewed for the targeted level
- Timed Mock Exam: original versioned paper, realistic timing and answer key
- Audio Skills: listening, dictation and transcript review
- Oral Simulation: scenarios, recorded answers, transparent feedback
- Writing Coach: task checklist, rubric-style feedback with human review for test calibration
- Mistake-to-Lesson: turn a missed question into personalized practice
- Progress: trends by skill; never label as an official Goethe grade

## 5. Content publishing workflow

1. Map competency to CEFR and the relevant official Goethe exam task format.
2. Write original material rather than modifying protected questions.
3. Attach rights and attribution metadata to all imported third-party material.
4. Have an experienced German teacher review grammar, vocabulary, ambiguity, level and scoring.
5. Have a proficient speaker review generated/recorded German audio.
6. Validate JSON and correct-answer logic in CI.
7. Import drafts into Neon `vercel-dev`, test with learners, then publish immutable approved versions to production.
8. Reassess materials when Goethe updates exam regulations or formats.

## 6. Four-week A1 exam preparation schedule (after completing A1 course)

**Week 1:** understand the A1 exam format, attempt sample questions by section, practise introduction and numbers.

**Week 2:** rotate listening and reading; make a vocabulary notebook for misheard words. Practise everyday messages and forms.

**Week 3:** practise short speaking exchanges daily; write and review one short message a day. Take a first full timed mock exam.

**Week 4:** focus on weaknesses; repeat an untouched official sample exam under exam conditions and seek human feedback on writing and speaking.

The schedule is a study suggestion, not a promise of passing. Learners still in A1.1 should use individual practice tasks without assuming they are ready for the full exam.
